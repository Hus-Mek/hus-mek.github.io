About

At Hawaz, a Riyadh consultancy that serves Saudi government entities, I automate the consulting work itself. Jobs that used to mean days of reading Arabic documents and filling spreadsheets now run end to end: my systems ingest hundreds of pages, score them against official frameworks, and hand the team a finished draft with the evidence behind every finding. Consultants spend their time on judgement and the client instead of data entry.

What that looks like in practice: an assessor that scores a government platform across all 20 axes of the DGA Digital Experience Maturity Index and checks itself against the official results; Rasid, which tracks 8,700+ Saudi government tenders, extracts their evaluation criteria, and ranks every opportunity so the bid team knows where to compete; and a review agent that reads a full contract or proposal in one pass and catches contradictions, date errors, and conflicting figures before the client does.

These systems are built to be trusted in government work. Code computes every number, the model does the judging, and any finding it can't quote from the source is dropped. They run on the firm's own GPUs with Saudi PII anonymized, so confidential client documents never go to a third-party cloud.

Before Riyadh I lived in the Netherlands, where I completed an M.Sc. in Applied Data Science at Utrecht University, wrote my thesis on satellite imagery at Readar, analyzed logistics data at Coty, and freelanced on data pipelines and scrapers. I'm British and Irish, and a native speaker of English and Arabic.
